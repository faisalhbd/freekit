## Best Practices for Image Compression

To get the most out of this tool and achieve the best results, follow these practical tips:

- **Start with the right format.** JPEG is generally best for photographs and images with complex color gradients. PNG is ideal for graphics, logos, and images requiring transparency. WebP often provides the best compression for both types.

- **Use moderate compression for web images.** A quality setting of 70-85% for JPEG and WebP files usually delivers substantial size reductions with no noticeable quality degradation on screens.

- **Compress at the final dimensions.** Resize your image to its intended display dimensions before compressing. Compressing an oversized image and then scaling it down wastes quality and file size.

- **Preview on multiple devices.** Check your compressed images on both desktop and mobile screens before publishing, as compression artifacts may be more visible on larger displays.

- **Keep original files.** Always save a copy of your original, uncompressed images before applying compression. This allows you to go back and re-compress at different quality levels later.

- **Batch-process similar images.** When compressing a set of images for the same purpose (such as a product gallery), use the same quality settings across all files to maintain visual consistency.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

Even experienced users sometimes fall into these common traps when compressing images:

- **Over-compressing.** Setting the quality too low can introduce visible artifacts, blurring, and banding that make images look unprofessional. Always use the preview to verify quality before downloading.

- **Compressing already-compressed images.** Applying lossy compression to an image that has already been compressed degrades quality further without providing much additional size reduction. Always work from the original file.

- **Ignoring format choice.** Using the wrong format can lead to unnecessarily large files. A PNG photograph can be 5-10 times larger than the same image saved as JPEG with equivalent visual quality.

- **Forgetting about transparency.** Converting a PNG with transparency to JPEG will replace transparent areas with a solid background. Use WebP or PNG when you need to preserve alpha channels.

- **Not testing on the target platform.** Different platforms and browsers render compressed images differently. What looks fine in your preview may appear different after being re-processed by a content management system or social media platform.
